UX / HCI Case Study

Digital Fridge Friend

An AI-powered recipe assistant designed to help users manage ingredients, receive personalized recipe suggestions, and reduce food waste.

Back to projects

Overview

Project summary

Digital Fridge Friend is a university HCI project focused on simplifying household ingredient management and recipe discovery through an intuitive, AI-supported app concept.

My Role

UX research, user journey, wireframing, prototyping, usability testing, data analysis

Tools

Figma, FigJam, SUS, think-aloud study, interviews

Context

Human-Computer Interaction university project at TU Berlin

Problem

The challenge

Many young adults struggle to decide what to cook when they have limited ingredients, little time, dietary restrictions, or fitness goals.

Manual recipe searches can be inefficient and overwhelming. The goal was to design an assistant that turns existing ingredients into relevant, personalized recipe suggestions.

Target Group

Who the app is for

The target audience was young adults with busy lifestyles, dietary restrictions, fitness goals, and an interest in healthier everyday cooking.

Design Process

From idea to prototype

01

User journey

I explored the user journey from recipe search to app usage, focusing on everyday cooking needs and user goals.

02

Low-fi and mid-fi sketches

I designed early app screens for login, recipe suggestions, profile settings, favorites, search, and navigation.

03

Prototype

I created a prototype with ingredient interactions, checkbox choices, camera access, and scrollable recipe instructions.

04

Testing and iteration

I evaluated the prototype using task completion, think-aloud feedback, interviews, SUS scores, and error rate analysis.

Research

Usability testing

The prototype was tested with four participants from the target group. They completed two tasks while sharing their thoughts through a think-aloud study.

After the tasks, participants took part in interviews and evaluated the app experience. The research focused on usability, efficiency, user satisfaction, task completion, errors, and navigation issues.

Findings

What the testing showed

Positive feedback

Participants liked the overall ease of use, layout, and helpfulness of the features.

Pain points

Some users struggled with navigation, especially around the sidebar and main menu.

Improvements

Users suggested clearer labels, better navigation, home buttons, and more ingredient information.

Iteration

Refining the prototype

Based on the research findings, the prototype was refined to improve clarity and navigation. Dedicated buttons were added to the home bar, the sidebar was repositioned, and visual clarity was improved.

Additional feature ideas included nutrient information and a recipe randomizer. Larger features such as a tracker or recipe-sharing system were left as future possibilities.

Reflection

What I learned

This project showed me how important clear navigation and feature clarity are for a smooth user experience.

I also learned that usability testing can reveal problems that are not obvious during the design phase, especially when users interact with the prototype in unexpected ways.
