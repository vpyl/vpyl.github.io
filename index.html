Featured Project

Digital Fridge Friend

Digital Fridge Friend explores how a simple digital assistant can help young adults reduce food waste, find suitable recipes, and make everyday cooking decisions easier.

My Role

UX research, user flows, wireframing, prototyping, concept development

Tools

Figma, FigJam, research documentation, usability testing

Context

University project focused on HCI, UX design, and user-centered thinking

Problem

The challenge

Many students and young adults struggle to decide what to cook, especially when they have limited ingredients, little time, or different dietary preferences. Ingredients are often forgotten, meals become repetitive, and food can go to waste.

The goal of this project was to design a friendly and practical assistant that helps users turn existing ingredients into meal ideas more easily.

Goal

What the product should do

  • Help users enter or track available ingredients.
  • Suggest recipes based on what users already have.
  • Consider preferences such as time, diet, and cooking habits.
  • Support grocery planning by showing missing ingredients.
  • Keep the experience simple, friendly, and easy to understand.

Design Process

From idea to prototype

01

Understanding the problem

I defined the everyday cooking problem and focused on users who want quick, realistic meal ideas.

02

User needs

I considered what users need before cooking: available ingredients, time, preferences, and clear recipe suggestions.

03

User flow

I mapped the main path from adding ingredients to receiving recipe suggestions and creating a grocery list.

04

Prototype

I created a visual prototype to explore the interaction flow and make the concept easier to communicate.

Solution

The concept

The final concept is a recipe assistant where users can add ingredients they already have at home. The system then suggests suitable recipes and helps users understand what they can cook immediately and what they may still need to buy.

The design focuses on reducing decision fatigue. Instead of asking users to search manually, the assistant gives them a smaller, more relevant set of options based on their real situation.

Reflection

What I learned

This project helped me understand how important simple interaction flows are. A recipe assistant should not feel like extra work. It should reduce effort and help users make faster decisions.

I also learned that UX design is not only about making screens look nice. It is about understanding user needs, structuring information clearly, and improving the design based on feedback.
